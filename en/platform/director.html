Management access to the system

The director's management box

This is not a duplicate of employees' workspaces, but a management layer that brings together the results of their work.

The director sees the event's status, not isolated tables

The dashboard combines data from various VA operational modules and presents them in the context of an individual performance. Before the event, sales, reservations and sales pace matter most. During the event, the focus shifts to actual attendance and admission checks. Afterward, the focus is on final results and channel effectiveness.

Performances by status

Events are not mixed into a single list. The dashboard lets managers work with them according to their life-cycle stage and the task at hand.

Presales

Sales pace, reservations, available seats, distribution channels and current hall occupancy.

In progress

Actual admissions, attendance rate, used and unused tickets, and the live operational picture.

Completed

Sales and attendance totals, channel results and data for subsequent analysis.

Postponed

A separate status for events whose date or terms have changed and require further action.

Cancelled

A separate view for events no longer included in the current operating schedule.

Archive

Past or deactivated performances remain accessible for historical records and analysis.

On screen: The event list is organised by status: on sale, completed, postponed, cancelled and archived.

Performances grouped by management status

On-sale, completed, postponed, cancelled and archived events are not mixed in one list.

What matters at each stage

Before the event

  • how many seats have been released for sale;
  • how many have been sold and reserved;
  • remaining inventory and hall occupancy;
  • sales by channel;
  • reservation status;
  • presale trends.

During the event

  • how many guests have entered;
  • how many have yet to enter;
  • actual attendance rate;
  • repeat scans;
  • QR errors and admission incidents;
  • current hall status.

After the event

  • final sales results;
  • actual attendance;
  • performance of different channels;
  • reservation conversion;
  • audience indicators;
  • data for future management decisions.

A ticket sold is not the same as a spectator in the hall

The dashboard uses not only sales data but also actual admission-control results. The director can therefore see real attendance rather than judging solely by the number of tickets sold.

Key management indicators

The dashboard does not replace the cashier, ticket office or marketer's workspaces. It brings their results together at the level the venue manager needs.

Sales and reservations

Current sales, active reservations and their share of overall ticket turnover.

Hall occupancy

Seats released for sale, sold seats, reserved seats and remaining availability.

Distribution channels

Sales split between online, box office and other connected channels.

Actual attendance

Scanner data shows how many spectators actually attended the event.

Audience engagement

Customer database and marketing data add audience context to ticketing figures.

Performance summary

Sales, attendance, channels and other key indicators form a single view of the event.

One performance — one management view

How it works in practice

The public page shows the main management views: sales for an individual performance and for the whole venue, compensation records and actual admission checks.

On screen: Single-performance metrics include ticket count, revenue, sales channels and ticket categories.

Sales for one performance

Ticket quantities, revenue, average transaction value, trends, sales channels and ticket types for one event.

On screen: The venue-wide summary presents sales and trends across the selected reporting period.

Venue-wide sales summary

Trends, revenue, average transaction value, channels and ticket mix in a broader management view.

On screen: Admission monitoring tracks accepted entries, repeat scans and validation outcomes.

Actual attendance

Issued tickets, admitted and absent attendees, attendance rate and breakdown by ticket type and sales channel.

On screen: Compensation records track replacement admission rights after an event is postponed or interrupted.

Compensation records

A separate management view shows compensation rights created and redeemed, outstanding obligations and links between performances.

The dashboard brings together results from other modules

Ticketing Core

Sales, reservations, remaining seats, box office and actual admission control.

Marketing

Acquisition channels, audiences, campaigns and accumulated marketing insights.

Customer Database

Purchase and attendance history, segments and renewed engagement with visitors.

The VA Management Dashboard is not just another workspace. It is the system's management layer, bringing together sales, attendance, channels, audience and special operations for individual performances and the venue as a whole.
